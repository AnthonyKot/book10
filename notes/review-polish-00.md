# Audit — `drafts/reader/00-poem.md`

## REQUIRED

- > “For a year I read the documents that do the choosing — the exam codifiers, the ministry programs, the state anthologies of twenty school systems — looking for the world in them: which foreign books each country lets into its classrooms, and why.”

  The supplied sources establish a twenty-file corpus and its document types, but not a year of reading. They also distinguish mandates, menus, anthologies, open formulas, and header-only files; “each country lets into its classrooms” makes those unlike doors sound uniformly national and operative. Remove or source the duration, and keep the document-level scope.

- > “Most rows in these documents ask that a book be read — discussed, examined, moved past. A few carry a different verb: учить наизусть, learn by heart — the state orders the text carried in the body.”

  No supplied instrument counts rows by the first set of activities, and “moved past” is not a documentary category. The second sentence also overstates force: Ukraine's five names come from one ministry-recommended alternative model program, while Kazakhstan's objective leaves the fragment to the pupil. State the narrower finding: a small set of rows carries explicit work-level by-heart wording.

- > “And when I filtered twenty systems for that one instruction, almost everything fell away. Sixteen countries, on paper, mandate no named text for memory at all. Four do.”

  `notes/instruments-2026-08-11.md` returns 23 rows in three systems—Belarus, Kazakhstan, and Ukraine—and explicitly returns zero for Russia under its then-current codifier corpus. The rebuilt `drafts/ru.md` later adds four domestic unit rows from the federal program. Four may become the updated result, but it is not yet the result of the cited cross-country instrument. Nor can the three header-only files be counted as national-system zeros. Reconcile the instrument before publishing either “sixteen” or “four.”

- > “Kazakhstan's by-heart list names twelve works at fifteen — from each, a passage to be held from memory.”

  `drafts/kz.md` supports twelve **rows** in Appendix 40 carrying objective 9.1.3.1, not a national twelve-work memorization list. Some rows bundle works, and the objective tells the pupil to find quotations or fragments rather than assigning one fixed passage from each work. Preserve row, appendix, stream, and excerpt precision.

- > “He is not on it. Not one Kazakh work is. All twelve are foreign: eleven Russian — Pushkin, Gogol, Dostoevsky, Tolstoy, Chekhov, an overcoat, a set of poor folk, a garnet bracelet — and Shakespeare's sonnets, the one non-Russian aboard, a tourist on the wrong train.”

  The sources establish that none of the twelve explicit by-heart **rows in Appendix 40** is domestic-coded; they do not establish that Kazakhstan has no Kazakh work memorized anywhere. “All twelve” again turns rows, some bundled, into works. Scope the finding to the four transcribed appendices and specifically to Appendix 40's written objective.

- > “Kazakh literature the program assigns for reading, discussion, essays; its by-heart instruction is written, eleven times out of twelve, against the literature of the empire the country used to belong to.”

  The first clause needs grammatical repair for straight-through readability. Factually, the file establishes eleven Russian-coded rows, not the historical claim that Kazakhstan “belonged to” the literature's empire or any reason for the placement. `drafts/kz.md` expressly says the program supplies no motive. Keep “eleven Russian-coded rows” and leave the imperial explanation as a clearly marked interpretation, if retained.

- > “the mandate is written in the federal teaching program that has bound every classroom since September 2023”

  `drafts/ru.md` gives the narrower legal scope: direct mandatory application in the compulsory part for organizations teaching a state-accredited basic general education program since 1 September 2023. “Every classroom” erases that scope and should be qualified.

- > “Three states, one Soviet schoolhouse, one pedagogic instrument in three hands — and it points at Europe in Ukraine, at the old metropole in Kazakhstan, and at home in Russia.”

  These are not one instrument. Ukraine has a named list in one recommended alternative model program; Kazakhstan attaches a pupil-selected-fragment objective to rows in one Russian-literature appendix; Russia combines an aggregate recitation outcome with unit-level wording in a binding federal program. Keep the Soviet-inheritance analogy as framing if desired, but do not present the three mechanisms or their force as one documentary fact. “Old metropole” is likewise interpretation, not a rationale stated by Kazakhstan's program.

- > “Two canons on one by-heart list, in two languages, in the same school year.”

  The instrument establishes four domestic-coded and two Russian-coded rows, and `notes/witness-by.md` establishes that Russian Literature was a separate subject with its own hours. The supplied sources do not establish the language in which each passage was memorized or actual pupil uptake. Recast this as two literature-coded sides in the program. Importantly, the draft makes no Belarusian personal-memory claim; preserve that boundary because the testimony about what the reader memorized remains pending.

- > “And the other sixteen? Their silence is the documents' silence — a table of mandates cannot see past the mandate.”

  “Other sixteen” inherits the unreconciled four-country count and wrongly converts header-only/open files into zeros. The limitation sentence is valid, but the group must be described as examined documents without a named work-level by-heart row, with incomplete files excluded rather than counted.

- > “In England, the exam board whose papers this book read makes its poetry exam closed-book, so quotation can only come from memory, while never once printing the instruction memorize; whatever memorizing happens hides in the exam's design. China mandates the practice and declines to sign the poem: recitation is required, foreign poetry is explicitly welcome in it, and the choice of texts is handed down to textbook editors and teachers.”

  The named source set supports only the filter rule that closed-book assessment, quotation requirements, aggregate outcomes, and unnamed recitation do not count as named work-level mandates. It does not contain enough England- or China-specific detail to verify these fuller claims. Add the relevant country-master support to the allowed source set or reduce both examples to the reconciled filter result. Do not imply actual memorizing from exam design.

- > “The four countries that write names into the instruction are the exception, and that is exactly why they matter: a named by-heart mandate is the one place in a school document where the state stops recommending and names the text a pupil is to carry.”

  The first clause depends on the unresolved Russia/instrument mismatch. The second contradicts the supplied force evidence: Ukraine's program is ministry-recommended, and Kazakhstan names a row while leaving the fragment to the pupil. Replace the uniform state-mandate claim with the exact shared property: explicit by-heart wording attached to a named work or unit.

- > “Decisions like that have signatures: an office, a document, a dated list. By the end of this book you will know where to look for yours — and you can put to it the question this book could never make a document answer: what did you want me to be?”

  The corpus can identify the office and document behind its sampled admissions; it cannot establish that the poem recalled by any particular reader came from that instrument, especially where teachers or pupils choose within menus. Keep the invitation to investigate, but do not promise a traceable signature for every reader's memory.

## SUGGESTED

- > “The second question the documents almost never answer.”

  This is a fair compact method limit, but the chapter repeats the same self-cross-examination in “cannot see past the mandate,” “no ministry can audit,” and “could never make a document answer.” Keep one or two formulations so the designed opening instrument does not reproduce the cold sweep's visible cannot-know refrain.

- > “There is a poem in you that starts itself.”

  Keep the second-person experiment. This chapter is expressly allowed second-person address as the book's opening instrument, so the general warning against uniform chapter landers does not apply here.

- > “учить наизусть, learn by heart”

  The AGENT contract otherwise passes: this is one short inline source-language phrase, translated immediately; titles do not create quotation freight; there are no running citations; and the chapter is readable in English straight through once the Kazakhstan fragment noted above is repaired.
