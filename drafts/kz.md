# Kazakhstan: Two Doors on One Corridor

Imagine a corridor with two classroom doors, and on both the same word: Literature. The
corridor is a construction — a way of holding one ministerial order's parallel
literature subjects side by side — but everything on the shelves behind the doors is
real, ninth grade, from the order's own lists.

Behind the left door — the Kazakh-medium stream — the year's list begins in the eleventh
century with Balasagun's «Құтты білік» and comes forward through the oratorical words of
Tole Bi, Kazybek Bi and Aiteke Bi, Bukhar Zhyrau, the aytys of Birzhan and Sara,
Makhambet, Walikhanov's «Ыстықкөл күнделігі», Zhansugirov's «Құлагер», Musirepov's
«Улпан ее имя» — the one title this list prints in Russian — out to Aytuly's «Бәйтерек».
Nineteen corpus rows. Not one is foreign.

Behind the right door — the Russian-medium stream — that stream's own literature subject
holds fourteen rows, and not one is domestic: Pushkin, Gogol twice, Dostoevsky, Tolstoy,
Chekhov, Leskov, Kuprin, and Shakespeare's sonnets. The subject called Literature is
wholly domestic in one room and wholly foreign-origin in the other, and both lists stand
in the same order, the same year.

Neither stream is missing its second literature. Elsewhere on the same timetable — the
order prints programs, not schedules — the streams trade languages: the Kazakh-medium
class takes Russian language-and-literature, the Russian-medium class Kazakh
language-and-literature. Every ninth-grader under this order carries two
literature-bearing lines; which two depends on one administrative fact — the language
the school teaches in. The window on the world's writing opens in a different wall for
each of them, and where it opens is the finding of this essay.

## The door: one order, four of its appendices

Everything above comes from one instrument: Order No. 399
of the Minister of Education (Просвещение) of the Republic of Kazakhstan, 16 September
2022, registered with the Ministry of Justice on 23 September 2022 under No. 29767, and
amended in 2023 by Order No. 328. It approves model curriculum programs as numbered
appendices, one per subject. Four of them are this essay's file:

- **Appendix 36 — "Казахская литература"**, for schools «с казахским языком обучения» —
  the left door.
- **Appendix 40 — "Русская литература"**, for schools «с русским языком обучения» — the
  right door.
- **Appendix 47 — "Казахский язык и литература"**, for schools «с неказахским языком
  обучения» — the right stream's second hour.
- **Appendix 48 — "Русский язык и литература"**, for schools «с нерусским языком
  обучения» — the left stream's second hour.

The same order approves more literature than this — Appendix 38 for the specialized
«Абай мектептері» network; Appendices 42, 44 and 46, Uyghur, Uzbek and Tajik
literature — none of it transcribed, so nothing below is a statement about the country's
programs as a whole. A Uyghur-medium ninth-grader falls outside the corridor's symmetry
entirely, taking Appendix 47 *and* Appendix 48 plus a literature of their own that this
file does not hold.

Force is legible from the work-column headings, and it differs by door. Appendices 36
and 40 head their columns «Изучаемые произведения» — works studied. Appendix 48 heads
its list «Изучаемые художественные произведения (по выбору)» — by choice — in both of
its printed plans. Appendix 47 will not hold still: its full-load plan heads the column
«Художественные произведения», its reduced-load plan heads the identical grade-9 list
«(по выбору)», and the file's ten Appendix 47 rows take their titles from one variant
and their force coding from the other — an unresolved mismatch this essay will not lean
on (Limits has the details). No hours are quoted anywhere here: the order prints
full-load and reduced-load variants and the file has no hours column. And none of these
rows is an exam item. These are ministry programs; a row's force is the force of a
printed program line, no more.

## What comes through, and where the window is cut

The repaired file holds 62 grade-9 rows: 35 domestic, 27 foreign-origin — 21 coded
`translated:russian`, four `translated:english`, two `translated:kyrgyz`, both Aitmatov.
Rows, not works: a single row can bundle several texts — Pushkin's lyric together with
«Цыганы», Chekhov's two teacher-chosen stories — so no count here is a count of works.
And the code records which literature a row belongs to, not the language of the copy a
class opens; what a Russian-medium classroom does with a Russian text is not the
column's business.

The distribution across the four doors is the finding, not the totals. The two subjects
that call themselves Literature split the shelf absolutely: Appendix 36's nineteen rows
all domestic, Appendix 40's fourteen all foreign-origin. The two second-hour courses
behave differently. Appendix 47 runs ten rows — nine domestic, from the Kul Tegin stele
to a poem on the Constitution, and one foreign: Asimov. Appendix 48 runs nineteen —
seven domestic, twelve foreign-origin (eight Russian, two English, two Kyrgyz), all
«по выбору». Which locates the thing worth locating: a Kazakh-medium ninth-grader's
window on the world's writing is cut in the Russian-language course, not in the subject
called Literature — and the list that holds it is headed "by choice," so what stands in
that window is offered rather than required. A Russian-medium ninth-grader's window IS
the subject called Literature, whose column is not a choice list — thirteen of its
fourteen rows are `mandatory`, with one Chekhov row the teacher chooses.

None of this is an openness measurement: nineteen against fourteen compares two
differently built subjects, not two quantities of the same thing. It is a statement
about where in the building each window was cut. And the file records that these works
are printed in these programs, nothing about why — inheritance, language policy, the
plain availability of translations and teachers all fit the same rows.

## The errand: an overcoat, carried by heart

The quarter headings supply the clearest teaching language used here, and the third and
fourth quarters of the two Literature subjects rhyme almost to the word. Appendix 36
gives its pupils «Секреты человеческой души» — secrets of the human soul; Appendix 40
gives its pupils «Тайны человеческой души» — mysteries of the same organ. The two
streams open it from opposite shelves: Kazakh literature reaches the soul through
Zhansugirov, Mailin, Musirepov, Aybergenov and Ongarsynova; Russian literature through
«Пиковая дама», Chekhov and Leskov. Two ministries of feeling inside one ministry,
running the same agenda with no author in common.

The quarter with no counterpart on the left side of the corridor is Appendix 40's
«Тема маленького человека» — the theme of the little man. Under it: Gogol's «Шинель»,
Dostoevsky's «Бедные люди», Tolstoy's «После бала», Kuprin's «Гранатовый браслет». An
overcoat, a clerk's letters, a flogging witnessed the morning after a ball, a minor
official's unanswerable love.

Take the overcoat down from the shelf, because the order asks more of it than reading.
The by-heart objective attached to this quarter — 9.1.3.1, quoted in full below —
requires a pupil to «самостоятельно находить в тексте и выразительно читать наизусть
цитаты, фрагменты, отражающие тематику произведения»: independently find in the text,
and expressively recite from memory, the lines that carry the work's theme. In «Шинель»
the theme has an address. A young clerk joins the office jokers tormenting Akaky
Akakievich until the copying clerk finally says «Оставьте меня, зачем вы меня обижаете?»
— leave me alone, why do you injure me? — and Gogol writes that inside those words the
young man heard others: «я брат твой» — I am your brother. The objective never names a
passage; the finding and the choosing are the pupil's. But a pupil hunting for "lines
reflecting the theme" under a heading that names the little man has this fragment as the
obvious candidate — the little man's one sentence of self-defense — and the feeling it
would import, on this essay's reading and not the program's stated result, is shame
before the small person. The order's verb for what a body should do with whatever
fragment is chosen is наизусть, by heart: a category of person, and a practised way of
feeling about that person, offered for carrying in memory as one quarter of a school
year.

Whose person, is worth a careful sentence. The corpus's repair pass recoded three of
the quarter's four rows to urban poverty, bureaucracy and military punishment — read
together (the essay's reading, assembled from those reconciled codings, not a ministry
rationale), the wounds three of these four books carry are the Russian Empire's own, and
the empire's own literature does the carrying. Gogol himself — Ukrainian-born,
Russian-language — arrives twice in Appendix 40 with no annotation of any kind. The
document places him and says nothing.

One objective states the whole triangulation outright. Appendix 40 requires comparing
«произведения (или фрагменты) русской, казахской и мировой литературы» — works of
Russian, Kazakh and world literature: three terms. Appendix 36's counterpart objective
has two: analysis «в сопоставлении с образцами казахской и мировой литературы» — against
examples of Kazakh and world literature. In the Kazakh literature subject, Russian
literature is not named as a separate term — if it enters that comparison at all, it
enters under world literature. In the Russian literature subject, it stands beside the
world by name.

## What a body is required to hold

Twelve rows in this file carry a memorization mandate, coded `excerpt`. All twelve are
in Appendix 40. All twelve are foreign-origin — eleven Russian and Shakespeare's
sonnets. No domestic row in the file carries one. The mandate is that same objective
9.1.3.1, attached to the first, second and fourth quarters of Appendix 40's grade-9
plan; the third quarter — Griboyedov, «Мертвые души» — does not carry it, which is why
those two rows read `no`.

That finding needs its brake fitted immediately. This is a fact about where an explicit
by-heart objective is *written*, not about what Kazakhstani children actually memorize.
Appendix 36 asks instead for the use of excerpts in creative work, and the
reconciliation pass stripped four earlier `excerpt` codings from its rows for exactly
that reason; recitation culture around Abai and Makhambet is not in this file, and its
absence from the file is not evidence of its absence from a classroom. What the file
supports is narrower and stranger: within these four appendices, the only lines
explicitly required to be carried in a ninth-grader's body all come from somewhere else.

## The one that came through the other door

Across the two Kazakh-language programs — Appendices 36 and 47 — a single foreign work
appears at grade 9, in the second-hour course, under the fourth-quarter theme
«Мир роботов и киборгов», the world of robots and cyborgs. The full-load plan prints it
as «Айзек Азимов "Мен-роботпын" (отрывок из сборника)»; the reduced-load plan prints
the same slot as «Айзек Азимов "Я -робот"». An American's robot stories, in the course a
Russian-medium child takes to learn the state language — and in the fuller of the two
plans, in Kazakh. Appendix 48, its mirror, is built on themes rather than authors —
under «Я и закон», me and the law, the listed text is «Ч. Айтматов. "Плаха" (фрагмент
самосуда Бостона)» — the fragment of Boston's vigilante justice, a man taking the law
into his own hands offered as the reading for a unit on legality — but everything on its
list stays «по выбору».

So set the two ninth-graders against each other one last time. One meets, in the subject
called Literature, nineteen domestic rows and no foreigner at all, and meets the world
through an optional Russian-course shelf. The other meets, in the subject called
Literature, fourteen rows of which every one is foreign-origin and twelve carry the
only explicit by-heart mandate in these four appendices, and meets Kazakh through a
course where the world arrives once, as Asimov. If your own timetable held two subjects
called Literature — two doors, two shelves, two languages — you already know that the
question "what did we read at school" has no single answer, and that which door you
remember depends on which door the system assigned you. Same order, same year, same
ministry. Two windows, cut in different walls of one house.

## Limits

- **Four appendices, not a country.** Appendix 38 (Abai schools) and Appendices 42, 44,
  46 (Uyghur, Uzbek, Tajik literature) were not transcribed.
- **Load variants are left out, and one is unresolved.** Only one variant per appendix
  was transcribed (Receipts names which). The grade-9 work lists agree across variants,
  but Appendix 47's two plans head their work column differently, and its ten rows mix
  one variant's titles with the other's force coding. Until re-receipted, ten of the
  file's thirty `choice-list` rows are unsettled, and no force claim about Appendix 47
  is made above. No hours are quoted: the variants carry different loads and the corpus
  has no hours field.
- **The file is not a complete transcription of Appendices 47 and 48.** Named in the
  document's grade-9 plans with no row in the file: App 48 — Paustovsky «Мещерская
  сторона», Rasputin «Прощание с Матерой», Seifullin «Кокшетау», Sasha Chorny
  «Диета»/«Зеркало», Akunin «Турецкий гамбит», Abai «Не будь падким на все», a
  first-quarter «Плаха» excerpt listed separately from the fourth-quarter one, and
  «Просчет Чингисхана» offered beside Shakhanov's Otrar poem; App 47 — Medeu Sarseke
  «Жетінші толқын», the «Бес арыс» excerpt, Abai «Адамның кейбір кездері». Among the
  missing foreign entries all are Russian except one — that first-quarter «Плаха»
  excerpt, Aitmatov, Kyrgyz under the corpus's own convention. So the file's Russian
  majority is not produced by under-collection of Russian rows — but 21-of-27, and every
  other count above, is a count of the repaired file, not of the order.
- **One grade, one year.** Grade 9, 2023 redaction. No reform history is receipted; no
  row can be dated as added or removed.
- **Programs, not an exam list.** `exam_status` codes a program's own column wording,
  not examinability; no count here is comparable with a codifier country's.
- **`origin` codes literature, not language or biography.** Russian-language works by
  authors coded `domestic` (Gundarev's «На земле Абая»), Kazakh works appearing in
  Appendix 48 under Russian titles (Shakhanov, Auezov), and both Aitmatov rows coded
  `translated:kyrgyz` all sit in one column; the corpus records no language of
  composition. The three author-identity facts used in prose are receipted above; no
  other biographical claim is made.
- **Memorization codings are conservative and one-sided by construction.** `excerpt`
  required an explicit by-heart mandate attached to the listed work; only Appendix 40
  attaches one. The finding concerns where such an objective is written — not what any
  classroom memorizes, and not who selects the passage.
- **No motive, anywhere.** The document offers no rationale for any shelf's
  composition; this essay makes no claim about why the 21 Russian rows are there.
- **Length.** ~3,030 words by `wc -w` against the 2,200 cap, of which the narrative
  body is ~2,020: the overrun is in the apparatus — four doors, an unresolved force
  mismatch, and a partial-transcription disclosure all require receipts — not in the
  story.
