# China: A Clause Left Open, and Four Guests

Open the People's Republic's compulsory-education Chinese-language standard at its first
appendix, and the window arrives before any foreign book does — inside a subordinate
clause, in the sentence that establishes the recitation mandate:

> 「义务教育阶段要求学生背诵古今优秀诗文，包括中国古代、现当代和外国优秀诗文，具体篇目可由教科书编者和语文教师推荐，这里仅推荐古诗文135篇（段）。」
> — "The compulsory-education stage requires students to recite excellent poetry and prose
> ancient and modern, including Chinese classical, Chinese modern and contemporary, and
> foreign excellent poetry and prose; the specific pieces may be recommended by textbook
> editors and language teachers; here only 135 pieces (passages) of classical poetry and
> prose are recommended." (Appendix 1, headnote, printed p. 58; my translation)

The clause opens the memory of a Chinese teenager to the poetry of the world. The list
underneath it then names classical Chinese poetry and prose, and hands the specific pieces
to editors and teachers. Of that list's Grade 7–9 segment — sixty rows in this table — the
number that are foreign is zero.

The foreigners are elsewhere in the same document, on a different shelf, doing a different
job. There are four of them.

## The door, which has two leaves

The defining document is 《义务教育语文课程标准（2022年版）》 — the Compulsory Education
Chinese Language Curriculum Standard, 2022 edition — issued by the Ministry of Education of
the People's Republic of China in April 2022. It governs one subject, 语文, across the whole
compulsory-education span; this table holds its lower-secondary rows. And it names books in
two appendices with unlike force.

Appendix 1 is the recitation leaf, quoted above: recommended pieces, of which sixty supply
this table's Grade 7–9 rows. Appendix 2 is the reading leaf, titled 关于课内外读物的建议 —
"Suggestions concerning reading materials in and out of class." It opens by requiring the
habit rather than the title: students are to 「多读书、读好书、读整本书」 — "read more, read
good books, read whole books" — and then it disclaims its own catalogue in the next
paragraph: 「下列推荐的读物仅为举例」 — "the reading materials recommended below are examples
only" (p. 64). What follows the disclaimer is a run of example lines, one per kind of
reading: fairy tales, fables, stories, poetry-and-prose works, long masterworks,
popular-science and science-fiction works.

Neither leaf makes a named work examinable, and the reconciliation pass therefore codes
every row in this table `choice-list` rather than `mandatory`. A row's force in China is not
examinability. It is a named example inside a mandated practice: the state requires that
reciting happen and that whole books be read, and leaves to editors and teachers which
texts do the work.

The nine whole-book rows cite a second leaf for their grade placement — the 名著导读
("guided reading of masterworks") units of the People's Education Press unified textbook —
whose pages are not in `resources/`, and Appendix 2 assigns no grades at all. So this essay
makes no use of the grade column: no ages, no ordering, no by-grade sequence. That is a
limit, not a footnote.

Four foreign works, then. This essay declines to read that as a measurement of welcome: an
example list inside a curriculum standard and an examination codifier are different
instruments, and their rows do not divide into each other. What the four rows carry is a
filing — and the filing is the document's own.

## The shelf, in the document's own categories

Sixty-nine rows: sixty-five domestic, four translated — two from English, one from French,
one from Russian. Every foreign row sits in Appendix 2's reading leaf, and the appendix
files them under two of its headings.

Under 科普科幻作品 — "popular-science and science-fiction works" — stands one line:
「科普科幻作品，如《十万个为什么》、儒勒·凡尔纳《海底两万里》等。」 (p. 64). Jules Verne
(1828–1905) enters beside 《十万个为什么》 — "A Hundred Thousand Whys." The table codes his
row `novel`, its subject era `19th-c-exploration`; the appendix puts him not among its
masterworks but on the line it shares with popular science. That heading covers science
fiction expressly, so nothing here says the ministry declines to call the book a novel. What
the filing does say is which shelf it was placed on and what stands next to it — and reading
that placement, which is this essay's reading and not a purpose the document states, a
submarine arrives as apparatus: a book admitted in the company of explanations.

The other three arrive on the 长篇名著 line — "long masterworks" — and the order of that
line is worth reading whole:

> 「长篇名著，如吴承恩《西游记》，施耐庵《水浒传》，老舍《骆驼祥子》，罗广斌、杨益言《红岩》，埃德加·斯诺《红星照耀中国》，斯威夫特《格列佛游记》，夏洛蒂·勃朗特《简·爱》，高尔基《童年》，奥斯特洛夫斯基《钢铁是怎样炼成的》等。」 (p. 64)

Charlotte Brontë (1816–1855) supplies 《简·爱》, coded `19th-c-victorian-class-gender`.
Nikolai Ostrovsky (1904–1936), whose novel this table codes `translated:russian`, supplies
《钢铁是怎样炼成的》, coded `russian-civil-war` — the one row on this shelf whose coded
subject is a war fought outside China.

And in the middle of that sentence, fifth of nine, stands the essay's jewel.

## Edgar Snow, the pivot

Read the 长篇名著 line as a sequence and it turns at one name. The first four titles are
Chinese: Wu Cheng'en, Shi Nai'an, Lao She, then Luo Guangbin and Yang Yiyan's 《红岩》. The
last four are foreign: Swift, Brontë, Gorky, Ostrovsky. The hinge between them is
埃德加·斯诺《红星照耀中国》 — Edgar Snow (1905–1972), whose book the table codes
`translated:english` and whose subject is China.

He is the only one of the four foreign rows the table does not code as fiction. Its `genre`
reads `essay`; its `subject_era` reads `red-army-long-march`. Three foreigners bring China a
submarine, a governess, and a Russian civil war. The fourth brings China an account of
China, written from outside it, in another language.

Three things about this row can be read closely, and only these three.

First, the heading it sits under. 名著 is canon vocabulary — the established, famous work.
The same appendix keeps a separate line for 诗歌散文作品, poetry-and-prose works, and that is
where Lu Xun's 《朝花夕拾》 stands. A foreign correspondent's reportage could have gone
there. It is on the masterworks line instead, wedged between the Chinese novels and the
foreign ones, and the table's `essay` coding is the record of the mismatch: a book of
reporting filed among the classics.

Second, the title. The Chinese does something the English gloss does not. *Red Star Over
China* is a preposition — a star hanging in place. 《红星照耀中国》 puts a verb in the middle:
照耀, to shine upon, to illuminate. The star acts, and the country is the grammatical object.

Third, the direction the appendix states for any further recommending a teacher does:

> 「根据教学需要，语文教师可按照《中小学生课外读物进校园管理办法》的规定，从中外各类优秀文学作品中选择合适的读物，特别是反映革命文化和社会主义先进文化的作品，向学生补充推荐。」
> — "According to teaching need, the language teacher may, in accordance with the
> *Measures for the Administration of Extracurricular Reading Materials Entering Primary and
> Secondary Schools*, select suitable reading matter from excellent literary works of all
> kinds, Chinese and foreign, especially works reflecting revolutionary culture and advanced
> socialist culture, and recommend them to students as supplements." (p. 64)

Snow's is the one foreign row in this table whose coded subject era belongs to that history.
The document shows the placement and states no reason for it; none is invented here. Read
together — the canon heading, the acting verb, the coded march — the arrangement reads as a
foreign witness admitted as a master, and what he is there to do is shine on the country's
own founding story. That reading is this essay's, taken off the document's own words. The
ministry's reason, if it has one, is not in the corpus.

## What is held, and what is only read

The table's `memorize` column reads `no` on all sixty-nine rows. The reconciliation pass
required an explicit mandate to learn a *named* text by heart before coding otherwise;
Appendix 1's headnote requires recitation in general, calls its pieces recommendations, sets
no quantity for grades 7–9, and leaves the specific pieces to editors and teachers. So no
single work in this table is a mandated memory.

But look at where the two leaves point. The recommended recitation shelf — Guan Ju, Mulan,
Du Fu's spring, Wen Tianxiang passing Lingding Ocean — is Chinese without exception across
its sixty Grade 7–9 rows. The whole-book reading shelf is nine rows, five Chinese and four
foreign. On this national recommendation list, then, no foreign work is named for memory,
and four are named for reading.

That is the whole of what the list can show, and it is worth saying what it cannot. The
clause that opened recitation to 外国优秀诗文 — foreign excellent poetry and prose — stands
open, and the same sentence hands the filling of it to textbook editors and language
teachers. Whatever they choose, this corpus never sees. A foreign poem may already be in
Chinese children's mouths by the route the document describes and declines to name.

If your own classroom set a list of whole books for the year, you can run the Chinese test
on it: which of them was written by an author from outside your country, and which of those
was about your country anyway. This list produces the second kind, once, on a shelf of nine
— and that is the reason a small shelf is worth an essay.

## Limits

- **Four is not this country's whole foreign presence.** Appendix 2's example lines name
  further foreign titles carrying no row here: 斯威夫特《格列佛游记》 (Swift) and
  高尔基《童年》 (Gorky) on the same 长篇名著 line, and 安徒生童话, 格林童话 and 《伊索寓言》
  (Andersen, Grimm, Aesop) on the fairy-tale and fable lines. Appendix 2 assigns none of them
  a grade, and this table holds lower-secondary rows only. The four rows are the works with a
  claimed lower-secondary textbook placement, not the ceiling of what the appendix names.
- **The quarantine, stated plainly.** Three rows were removed before this essay was written:
  Jean-Henri Fabre's 《昆虫记》, Fu Lei's 《傅雷家书》, and Wu Jingzi's 《儒林外史》. Each was
  searched for under Chinese and Latin author and title variants across every saved defining
  document and found in none, and no textbook receipt for any of them sits in `resources/`.
  They stay out until a work-level receipt exists, and no count above includes them. None of
  this is evidence that these works are absent from Chinese classrooms — only that this
  corpus cannot receipt them.
- **The nine whole-book rows lean on an unsaved receipt.** Their titles are corroborated by
  Appendix 2, which is saved; their *grade placements* come from the PEP unified textbook,
  whose pages are not in `resources/`, and Appendix 2 assigns no grades. The `grade` column
  is therefore unreceipted, and nothing above rests on it — no age, no year-by-year sequence,
  no by-grade payoff.
- **Force is coded, not measured.** All 69 rows now read `exam_status` `choice-list`:
  Appendix 2 says of its catalogue 「仅为举例」 — "examples only" — and Appendix 1 calls its
  pieces recommendations, so no row in this table records examinability. The coding says a
  work is named in a binding document, not that a student can be tested on it. No
  cross-country comparison of row totals is offered above; the door types are not
  commensurable.
- **The recitation clause's delegated choices are unobserved.** Appendix 1's headnote puts
  foreign poetry and prose inside the general recitation requirement and then leaves the
  specific pieces to textbook editors and language teachers. This corpus records only the
  nationally recommended pieces. Editor- and teacher-selected recitation texts — where
  foreign work may well already sit — are outside it entirely, so the finding above is about
  what this list names, not about what Chinese pupils recite.
- **One slice, one subject.** The 2022 standard as of a 2026-08-10 retrieval, covering 语文
  (Chinese language and literature) only. Foreign works read inside the English-language
  subject, in provincial supplementary lists, or in senior secondary (a different standard)
  are outside this table entirely.
- **Motive is not in the corpus.** The appendix names a direction for supplementary
  recommendation — 革命文化和社会主义先进文化 — and it places Snow's book on the masterworks
  line. It does not connect the two, and neither does this essay; the errand read off the
  heading, the verb, and the coded subject era above is this essay's reading of the
  document's language, not a purpose the ministry states. Placement is the finding.
