The corrected maximum is present: *Gulliver's Travels* is four of seventeen, and the six works at three of seventeen match Addendum 2. The national-poet and four-file memorization counts also match the memo. The chapter nevertheless needs repair for force, scope, cold-sweep, and story-spine violations.

## REQUIRED

1. > “What the rows can do is be compared. Compared, they refuse the premise this book started with.”

   The rows remain the acting protagonist; elsewhere the tables “refuse,” “single,” and “sign.” That is the prohibited document/table spine, not a pupil, physical book, work crossing a border, or author moving through doors.

2. > “And no two of the seven hand a pupil the same thing.”
   >
   > “Seven admissions, seven different objects.”

   A row proves document admission, not that a pupil was handed or read the work. “Seven different objects” also distorts the data: several files admit multiple Shakespeare objects, and Kazakhstan and Vietnam both name *Romeo and Juliet*. The seven country-level combinations differ; they are not seven single works.

3. > “Kazakhstan assigns the sonnets — an excerpt to be learned by heart — and Romeo and Juliet.”

   This flattens unlike force. The sonnets row is mandatory and marked for excerpt memorization; *Romeo and Juliet* is a choice-list row. The sentence makes them sound equally assigned.

4. > “Ukraine puts one sonnet into memory, the pupil choosing between 116 and 130.”

   The current row supports a by-heart mandate and a choice between the two sonnets, but neither the memo nor the table identifies the pupil as the chooser.

5. > “Drop from authors to works and the ceiling drops with you.”

   This is second person. The cold-sweep budget is spent, so even this figurative use violates the ruling.

6. > “Russia requires chapters of it, the pupil's choice, at twelve.”

   The rebuilt Russian table says “chapters by choice” and does not identify the pupil as the chooser. “Required at grade six” is supported; the chooser is invented.

7. > “Four countries, and the book is never the same size twice: a menu option, an anthology entry, one island of four, a fistful of chapters.”
   >
   > “If there is a world canon, this is it — a satire about a man repeatedly washed up in countries built to the wrong scale, admitted by four of seventeen systems, between the ages of twelve and seventeen, never in the same amount.”

   Addendum 2 supports the four admissions and their different administrative forms, but not “never the same size” or “never in the same amount.” The Bolivian row establishes anthology presence, not how much text is printed, and the unspecified Russian chapters are not proved different in extent from Ukraine's Part One.

8. > “The summit of the world's shared shelf, measured across twenty school systems, is one eighteenth-century satire and a handful of books that three ministries could agree on.”

   The shared-work recount is across seventeen populated files, not twenty comparable school systems. The files include a state list, an exam-board list, an exemplar appendix, and anthology entries; “three ministries” is false, while “could agree on” invents agreement from recurrence.

9. > “And England's occupant is Shakespeare himself: six rows on the exam board's lists, twice his nearest rival — and not one of the board's seventy-three rows is coded as a translation.”
   >
   > “The world's most-admitted foreigner holds exactly one domestic chair, his own, in the one table that admits no foreigners at all.”

   Six versus three and zero translation-coded rows are facts about the AQA file. “England's occupant” widens one exam board into a national claim, and “admits no foreigners at all” hardens a coding result into exclusion. The rejected-claims register expressly forbids that move.

10. > “Admission is the shallow register. The deep one is memory — the texts a system does not just assign but signs into a child by heart, the poem that still starts itself twenty years later.”
   >
   > “Even at the deepest layer the corpus has, where a state chooses the words a body will carry, there is no shared text, no common poem, nothing every child ends up holding.”

   The 27 work-specific rows support named by-heart instructions, not successful memorization, twenty-year retention, or an outcome for every child. This converts a document mandate into classroom and bodily uptake, which the memo explicitly refuses.

11. > “Kazakhstan teaches The Overcoat as foreign literature and has pupils lift its passages by heart.”

   The table supports foreign coding and an excerpt-memorization mandate. “Teaches” and “has pupils” state classroom uptake; keep the actor and force at the program level.

12. > “Five systems needed a Gogol, and each built the one it needed.”

   “Needed” is an invented causal errand. The five placements prove admission and differing status, not why any system selected Gogol; the refusal register rules out motive from admission alone.

13. > “The systems that memorize face four different compass points, and the nearest thing to a common author arrives as seven different objects in seven different years of a child's life.”

   Systems issue memorization mandates; the corpus does not show that the systems or children actually memorize. “Seven different objects” repeats the object-count distortion, and “seven different years” is unsupported: the files contain overlapping grade bands, while Russia alone admits Shakespeare in two grades.

14. > “No book is everywhere. No office is universal.”
   >
   > “Given all of literature to choose from, twenty ministries built twenty different worlds and each called its world the world. There is no world canon. There are only windows — and a country on the near side of each one, deciding what a fifteen-year-old will see.”

   This is the largest scope failure. The authority supports no work common to the seventeen populated current files and no universal national-poet slot under the memo's operational test. It does not support claims about everywhere, twenty ministries, unconstrained choice from all literature, institutional self-conception, or what a fifteen-year-old actually sees. Three files are header-only, and several populated files are subnational, optional, or non-ministerial. The global title may remain an argument, but these sentences state it as a proved national and classroom fact.
