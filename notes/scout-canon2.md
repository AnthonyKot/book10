# School Literature Canon Scouting Report — Wave 2 (Book 10)

This report scouts the official source documents defining the examineable school literature canon for 14–16-year-olds across 10 national school systems in **Wave 2** (Latin America ×3, Asia ×3, and Multilingual/Federal windows ×4). 

Per [`CONTEXT.md`](../CONTEXT.md), the primary subject is **"the window"** — the foreign/translated literature each country admits into its literature classroom, measured against the enforceable **LIST** (exam codifiers, prescribed text specifications, state anthologies, or university vestibular lists).

## Key Synthesis Questions

### (a) Does the system mandate memorization of specific texts?
- **Vietnam (VN)**: **Yes**. Mandatory memorization (*học thuộc lòng*) of key classical Sino-Vietnamese poetry and foundational patriotic speeches (*Nam quốc sơn hà*, *Bình Ngô đại cáo*, *Truyện Kiều*, *Tuyên ngôn Độc lập*).
- **Mongolia (MN)**: **Yes**. Mandatory memorization (*цэээжлэх*) of designated classic stanzas (B. Yavuuhulan, D. Natsagdorj's *My Native Land*, traditional epic excerpts).
- **Japan (JP)**: **Partial / Recommended**. MEXT Courses of Study require recitation and memorization (*暗誦 / 朗読*) of classical Japanese/Chinese poetry (*Hyakunin Isshu*, Haiku/Tanka, Tang poetry by Li Bai and Du Fu, *Lunyu* excerpts).
- **Mexico (MX), Bolivia (BO), Brazil (BR), Belgium (BE), Australia (AU), Switzerland (CH), Norway (NO)**: **No statutory memorization list**. Evaluation focuses on analytical essay writing, thematic critique, and literary context rather than oral recitation by heart.

### (b) Is there a national-literature vs. world-literature subject split (like Ukraine's separate «Зарубіжна література»)?
- **No country in Wave 2 maintains an entirely separate, standalone mandatory subject titled "World Literature"** identical to Ukraine's administrative split.
- **Vietnam (VN)**: Structurally mandates foreign literature within the single *Môn Ngữ văn* subject via a mandatory selection quota (*Văn học nước ngoài*) requiring at least one text each from 8 foreign traditions (UK, France, USA, Greece, Russia, Japan, China, India).
- **Bolivia (BO), Japan (JP), and Mongolia (MN)**: Explicitly label foreign literature as a distinct curricular sub-unit inside the core language arts subject (*Comunicación y Lenguajes* in BO, *Kokugo* in JP, *Уран зохиол* in MN).
- **Brazil (BR - FUVEST)**: Enforces a **strict inverted split**: the examineable literature exam is restricted entirely to *Língua Portuguesa e Literaturas de Língua Portuguesa* (Brazil, Portugal, Lusophone Africa). Non-Portuguese foreign literature in translation is **0% examineable** on FUVEST.
- **Belgium (BE), Australia (AU), Switzerland (CH), Norway (NO)**: Foreign literature in translation is integrated directly into the main national language subject (*Nederlands*, *Français*, *English*, *Deutsch*, *Norsk*) as option choices without an administrative subject split.

## Country Scouting Reports

### 1. Mexico (MX)

1. **Exact Document**: *Programa de Estudio para la Educación Secundaria: Programa Sintético de la Fase 6 (Lenguajes)* & *Libros de Texto Gratuitos (Colección Nanahuatzin - 3er grado de secundaria)*.
   - **Issuing Body**: Secretaría de Educación Pública (SEP) & Comisión Nacional de Libros de Texto Gratuitos (CONALITEG).
   - **Most Recent Year**: 2022 (Plan de Estudios) / 2023–2024 (Phase 6 textbook rollout).
   - **Working URL**: [https://educacionbasica.sep.gob.mx/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Programa_Sintetico_Fase_6.pdf](https://educacionbasica.sep.gob.mx/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Programa_Sintetico_Fase_6.pdf) & [https://libros.conaliteg.gob.mx/](https://libros.conaliteg.gob.mx/).
2. **Format**: Teacher-choice framework under the *Nueva Escuela Mexicana (NEM)* ("autonomía profesional y codiseño analítico") centered around state-issued mandatory free textbooks (*Libros de Texto Gratuitos*).
3. **Approximate Size & Foreign Share**:
   - ~25–35 works/excerpts across Grade 3 Secundaria (age 14–15).
   - Foreign share: ~10–15% non-Hispanic foreign literature. The curriculum prioritizes Mexican indigenous languages/literature and Latin American authors (Gabriel García Márquez, Horacio Quiroga, Pablo Neruda, Mario Benedetti) alongside Spanish classics (Cervantes, Lorca). Non-Hispanic translated works (Shakespeare, Poe) appear only as optional project references. The SEP framework explicitly categorizes texts by community problem-solving themes rather than national origin.
4. **Concrete Finding**: The 2023 rollout of the *Nueva Escuela Mexicana (NEM)* textbooks eliminated standalone literature textbooks (previously *Español y Literatura*) in favor of integrated project books (*Colección Nanahuatzin*), sparking a nationwide political and legal battle. Opposition groups and the *Unión Nacional de Padres de Familia* obtained court injunctions in Chihuahua and Coahuila, alleging that the abolition of traditional literary canons and grammar units degraded basic educational standards.
   - **Source URL**: [https://www.eluniversal.com.mx/nacion/la-sep-y-la-polemica-por-los-nuevos-libros-de-texto-gratuitos-claves-para-entender/](https://www.eluniversal.com.mx/nacion/la-sep-y-la-polemica-por-los-nuevos-libros-de-texto-gratuitos-claves-para-entender/).
5. **Feasibility Grade**: **MEDIUM**. The SEP *Programa Sintético* sets open learning processes (PDAs), but the state *CONALITEG* textbook TOC provides a concrete proxy for examineable text selections.

### 2. Bolivia (BO)

1. **Exact Document**: *Planes y Programas de Educación Secundaria Comunitaria Productiva: Campo Comunidad y Sociedad - Comunicación y Lenguajes* & *Texto de Aprendizaje: Comunicación y Lenguajes (4to, 5to y 6to de Secundaria)*.
   - **Issuing Body**: Ministerio de Educación del Estado Plurinacional de Bolivia (MINEDU).
   - **Most Recent Year**: 2023 / 2024 edition (under Resoluciones Ministeriales 1040/2022 and 0001/2024).
   - **Working URL**: [https://www.minedu.gob.bo](https://www.minedu.gob.bo).
2. **Format**: Statutory curriculum standards PDF + mandatory State-published *Textos de Aprendizaje* (grade-by-grade printed anthologies).
3. **Approximate Size & Foreign Share**:
   - ~25–35 works across 4to–6to Secundaria (ages 14–16).
   - Foreign share: ~10–15% non-Latin American translated literature. Indigenous pre-colonial literature (*Ollantay*, *Popol Vuh*, Quechua/Aymara oral traditions) and Bolivian national classics (Nataniel Aguirre's *Juan de la Rosa*, Adela Zamudio, Alcides Arguedas' *Raza de bronce*, Augusto Céspedes' *Sangre de mestizos*, Edmundo Paz Soldán) form ~60–70% of the text corpus. Latin American Boom/Modernism (García Márquez, Rulfo, Neruda, Darío) make up ~20–25%. European/Universal classics (Machiavelli, Shakespeare, Cervantes) make up ~10–15%. The document explicitly tags units as *Literatura Boliviana*, *Literatura Latinoamericana*, and *Literatura Universal*.
4. **Concrete Finding**: Under the *Ley 070 Avelino Siñani - Elizardo Pérez* decolonization reform and the 2023 curriculum revision, MINEDU mandated that secondary literature must center indigenous worldview narrative structures (*cosmovisión originaria*) and anti-colonial Bolivian social prose, explicitly shifting classroom hours away from traditional Eurocentric canons toward plurinational indigenous texts.
   - **Source URL**: [https://www.minedu.gob.bo/index.php/nota-de-prensa-2023/6314-minedu-socializa-los-nuevos-contenidos-curriculares-2023](https://www.minedu.gob.bo/index.php/nota-de-prensa-2023/6314-minedu-socializa-los-nuevos-contenidos-curriculares-2023).
5. **Feasibility Grade**: **MEDIUM**. The curriculum is published centrally, and official MINEDU *Textos de Aprendizaje* PDFs contain full lesson text units.

### 3. Brazil (BR)

1. **Exact Document**:
   - *Regulatory Framework*: *Base Nacional Comum Curricular (BNCC) – Ensino Médio*.
   - *Enforceable Exam List*: *Lista de Obras de Leitura Obrigatória da FUVEST (Vestibular USP)* & *Lista de Obras de Leitura Obrigatória da UNICAMP*.
   - **Issuing Body**: Ministério da Educação (MEC) / Fundação Universitária para o Vestibular (FUVEST - USP) & COMVEST (Unicamp).
   - **Most Recent Year**: 2024 / 2025 / 2026 published lists.
   - **Working URL**: [https://www.fuvest.br/vestibular-fuvest-obras-literarias/](https://www.fuvest.br/vestibular-fuvest-obras-literarias/) (FUVEST official) & [http://basenacionalcomum.mec.gov.br/](http://basenacionalcomum.mec.gov.br/) (BNCC).
2. **Format**: The national BNCC is a pure competency framework without fixed book titles. The enforceable *vestibular* list (FUVEST) is a strict, statutory 9-book list updated annually on a rotating cycle.
3. **Approximate Size & Foreign Share**:
   - Size: Exactly 9 mandatory works for FUVEST.
   - Foreign share: **0% non-Portuguese foreign literature**; **~22% Lusophone foreign literature** (1–2 works per year from Portugal or Lusophone Africa, e.g. Fernando Pessoa's *Mensagem*, Luís Bernardo Honwana's *Nós matamos o cão tinhoso!*, Pepetela, Paulina Chiziane). Because FUVEST tests *Língua Portuguesa e Literaturas de Língua Portuguesa*, non-Portuguese translated works (Shakespeare, Dostoevsky) are **0% examineable** on the literature paper.
4. **Concrete Finding**: For the **FUVEST 2026 examination**, the university board enacted a historic reform by designating a reading list composed **100% of female authors** writing in Portuguese (Nísia Floresta, Narcisa Amália, Julia Lopes de Almeida, Rachel de Queiroz, Sophia de Mello Breyner Andresen, Lygia Fagundes Telles). Furthermore, FUVEST's 2027–2030 long-term plan explicitly mandates the introduction of Indigenous Brazilian authors (Ailton Krenak) and Afro-Brazilian classics (Carolina Maria de Jesus).
   - **Source URL**: [https://jornal.usp.br/universidade/fuvest-divulga-lista-de-obras-literarias-obrigatorias-para-o-vestibular-2026/](https://jornal.usp.br/universidade/fuvest-divulga-lista-de-obras-literarias-obrigatorias-para-o-vestibular-2026/).
5. **Feasibility Grade**: **EASY** for FUVEST (an exact, auditable 9-row table with complete receipt history); **HARD** if attempting to construct a single list from BNCC alone.

### 4. Vietnam (VN)

1. **Exact Document**: *Chương trình Giáo dục phổ thông Môn Ngữ văn (Ban hành kèm theo Thông tư 32/2018/TT-BGDĐT)* & *Bộ sách giáo khoa Ngữ văn 9/10 (Cánh diều, Kết nối tri thức với cuộc sống, Chân trời sáng tạo)*.
   - **Issuing Body**: Bộ Giáo dục và Đào tạo (Ministry of Education and Training - MOET Vietnam).
   - **Most Recent Year**: 2018 (Curriculum Framework) / 2024 (Grade 9/10 rollout).
   - **Working URL**: [https://moet.gov.vn](https://moet.gov.vn) & [https://thuvienphapluat.vn/van-ban/Giao-duc/Thong-tu-32-2018-TT-BGDDT-Chuong-trinh-giao-duc-pho-thong-403998.aspx](https://thuvienphapluat.vn/van-ban/Giao-duc/Thong-tu-32-2018-TT-BGDDT-Chuong-trinh-giao-duc-pho-thong-403998.aspx).
2. **Format**: Statutory PDF curriculum Circular specifying 6 mandatory national masterworks (*Tác phẩm bắt buộc*) + a mandatory foreign literature selection quota (*Văn học nước ngoài*) across approved state textbook sets.
3. **Approximate Size & Foreign Share**:
   - ~40–50 prescribed texts across Grades 9–10.
   - Foreign share: ~20–25%. The 2018 Curriculum mandates 6 core national texts (*Nam quốc sơn hà*, *Hịch tướng sĩ*, *Bình Ngô đại cáo*, *Truyện Kiều*, *Văn tế nghĩa sĩ Cần Giuộc*, *Tuyên ngôn Độc lập*). For foreign literature, Circular 32/2018 explicitly mandates that textbook anthologies MUST include at least one representative work from **8 specific foreign national traditions**: English, French, American, Greek, Russian, Japanese, Chinese, and Indian literature (e.g., Pushkin, Lu Xun, Shakespeare, Andersen, Saint-Exupéry, Hemingway, Tagore, Homer).
4. **Concrete Finding**: In 2022–2024, MOET issued official guidance prohibiting exam boards from using text passages printed directly inside students' textbooks for high-stakes Grade 9 graduation and Grade 10 entrance examinations. This landmark reform aimed to eliminate rote memorization (*học tủ/học vẹt*), forcing students to analyze unseen texts while choosing between three competing approved textbook series (*Cánh diều*, *Kết nối tri thức*, *Chân trời sáng tạo*).
   - **Source URL**: [https://moet.gov.vn/tintuc/Pages/tin-tong-hop.aspx?ItemID=8012](https://moet.gov.vn/tintuc/Pages/tin-tong-hop.aspx?ItemID=8012).
5. **Feasibility Grade**: **EASY**. The 6 national statutory works are fixed by law, and textbook TOCs provide precise lists for the 8 mandated foreign traditions.

### 5. Japan (JP)

1. **Exact Document**: *中学校学習指導要領（平成29年告示）国語* (Courses of Study for Lower Secondary School: Japanese) & MEXT-approved Grade 3 Kokugo textbooks (文部科学省検定済教科書 中学校国語 3年, e.g., *Mitsumura Tosho*, *Tokyo Shoseki*).
   - **Issuing Body**: 文部科学省 (Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology - MEXT).
   - **Most Recent Year**: 2017 (Course of Study) / 2021 (Reiwa 3 textbook adoption) / 2025 (Reiwa 7 revision).
   - **Working URL**: [https://www.mext.go.jp/a_menu/shotou/new-cs/1387018.htm](https://www.mext.go.jp/a_menu/shotou/new-cs/1387018.htm) & [https://www.mitsumura-tosho.co.jp/](https://www.mitsumura-tosho.co.jp/).
2. **Format**: National MEXT framework setting statutory hours/competencies, implemented through commercial MEXT-inspected textbooks (*検定済教科書*).
3. **Approximate Size & Foreign Share**:
   - ~15–20 works per Grade 3 textbook anthology.
   - Foreign share: ~20–30%. Classical Chinese literature (*Kanbun*: *Lunyu* / Confucius, Tang poets Li Bai and Du Fu) forms a mandatory traditional pillar. Modern foreign translated literature includes Lu Xun's *My Old Home* (《故郷》 / *Kokyo*, translated by Takeuchi Yoshimi), alongside Western short fiction and essays.
4. **Concrete Finding**: Lu Xun's *My Old Home* (《故郷》) has been published continuously in virtually **every MEXT-approved 3rd-year junior high school Kokugo textbook for over 70 years** (since 1953). It serves as the primary literary bridge between Chinese modernism and Japanese public education, surviving multiple geopolitical changes and curriculum revisions.
   - **Source URL**: [https://www.mitsumura-tosho.co.jp/chronicle/kokugo/chugaku/c_s28.html](https://www.mitsumura-tosho.co.jp/chronicle/kokugo/chugaku/c_s28.html).
5. **Feasibility Grade**: **EASY**. MEXT standards and Mitsumura Tosho textbook TOCs provide a clean, highly standardized text list.

### 6. Mongolia (MN)

1. **Exact Document**: *Суурь боловсролын сургалтын хөтөлбөр: Уран зохиол (8-9 дүгээр анги)* (Basic Education Literature Curriculum: Grades 8–9) & *Уран зохиол IX* (Grade 9 State Literature Textbook).
   - **Issuing Body**: Боловсрол, Шинжлэх Ухааны Яам (Ministry of Education and Science) / Боловсролын Ерөнхий Газар (General Authority for Education).
   - **Most Recent Year**: 2019 (Core Curriculum update) / 2023–2024 (Digital state textbook edition via `medle.mn`).
   - **Working URL**: [https://medle.mn](https://medle.mn) & [https://econtent.edu.mn](https://econtent.edu.mn).
2. **Format**: Statutory curriculum framework PDF + State-issued unified national textbook (*Уран зохиол 9*).
3. **Approximate Size & Foreign Share**:
   - ~25–35 works across Grades 8–9.
   - Foreign share: ~25–35%. Mongolian literature features nomadic epic traditions (*Secret History of the Mongols*, *Geser*, *Jangar*) and 20th-century national classics (D. Natsagdorj, B. Rinchen, Ch. Lodoidamba). The curriculum's post-Soviet legacy retains a substantial share of Russian classics (Pushkin, Tolstoy, Chekhov) alongside Eastern classics (ancient Indian *Subhashita* proverbs, Tang poetry) and Western classics (Shakespeare, Andersen, Hugo). Foreign literature is explicitly grouped under *Гадаадын уран зохиол / Дэлхийн сонгодог*.
4. **Concrete Finding**: Following the 1990 Democratic Revolution, Mongolia overhauled its literature curriculum to purge Soviet Marxist-Leninist political texts and restore long-suppressed national nomadic epics (*The Secret History of the Mongols*, Buddhist wisdom literature). However, the ministry deliberately preserved a non-ideological window of classic Russian literature (Pushkin, Chekhov) alongside newly translated Western and Asian authors.
   - **Source URL**: [https://sudalgaa.gov.mn](https://sudalgaa.gov.mn).
5. **Feasibility Grade**: **MEDIUM**. Official state textbooks and curriculum guides are fully digitized on `medle.mn` and `econtent.edu.mn`.

### 7. Belgium (BE)

1. **Exact Document**:
   - **Flemish Community (Vlaanderen)**: *Eindtermen Secundair Onderwijs: Nederlands (2e en 3e graad)* & *Leerplannen Katholiek Onderwijs Vlaanderen / GO! Onderwijs*.
   - **French Community (Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles - FWB)**: *Référentiel de Français: Tronc Commun (3e secondaire / S3)* & *Programmes d'Études de Français (2e/3e degré)*.

   - **Most Recent Year**: 2021–2023 (Flemish *Eindtermen* litigation) / 2022–2024 (FWB *Pacte pour un Enseignement d'Excellence* rollout).
   - **Working URL**: [https://www.vlaanderen.be/onderwijs-en-vorming/onderwijsdoelen](https://www.vlaanderen.be/onderwijs-en-vorming/onderwijsdoelen) & [http://www.wallonie-bruxelles-enseignement.be](http://www.wallonie-bruxelles-enseignement.be).
2. **Format**: Competency-based frameworks issued separately by each linguistic community; neither community mandates a single nationwide or community-wide 30-book list.
3. **Approximate Size & Foreign Share**:
   - Size: 0 statutory central titles (~15–20 titles per year selected by school networks or individual teachers).
   - Foreign share:
     - *Flemish Community*: ~30–40% of literature studied in *Nederlands* is translated foreign literature (English, French, German, world classics) alongside Dutch/Flemish authors (Multatuli, Boon, Claus).
     - *French Community (FWB)*: Heavy emphasis on French national classics (Molière, Hugo, Camus) and Francophone Belgian authors (Simenon, Nothomb); translated non-French literature makes up ~15–25%.
4. **Concrete Finding**: In June 2022, the Belgian Constitutional Court (*Grondwettelijk Hof*) struck down the new Flemish Secondary Education Attainment Targets (*Eindtermen 2e en 3e graad*) following a lawsuit filed by Catholic Education Flanders (*Katholiek Onderwijs Vlaanderen*). The court ruled that the government's overly detailed attainment targets violated freedom of education (Article 24 of the Constitution) by overly restricting school network autonomy.
   - **Source URL**: [https://www.const-court.be/public/n/2022/2022-082n.pdf](https://www.const-court.be/public/n/2022/2022-082n.pdf).
5. **Feasibility Grade**: **HARD**. Two distinct linguistic communities without a central statutory list; evaluation requires network *leerplannen* or textbook series (*Nieuw Netwerk* / *Parcours*).

### 8. Australia (AU)

1. **Exact Document**: *English Stage 6 – Prescriptions: Modules, Electives and Texts (2019–2026 / extended to 2030)* (NSW HSC Prescribed Texts List) & *English Stage 5 (Years 9–10) Syllabus*.
   - **Issuing Body**: NSW Education Standards Authority (NESA), New South Wales State Government. (Note: Australia is state-based; Victoria uses VCAA VCE list, Queensland uses QCAA).
   - **Most Recent Year**: 2019–2026 prescriptions (extended by NESA to apply through 2030).
   - **Working URL**: [https://educationstandards.nsw.edu.au/wps/portal/nesa/11-12/stage-6-learning-areas/stage-6-english/english-prescriptions-2019-2026](https://educationstandards.nsw.edu.au/wps/portal/nesa/11-12/stage-6-learning-areas/stage-6-english/english-prescriptions-2019-2026).
2. **Format**: Official PDF list of mandatory prescribed set-texts categorized by course (Standard, Advanced, Extension, EAL/D) and module.
3. **Approximate Size & Foreign Share**:
   - ~100 prescribed titles across all Stage 6 courses (schools choose 4 set works per course).
   - Foreign share: ~50–60% of the prescribed catalog consists of British (Shakespeare, Jane Austen, George Orwell, T.S. Eliot) and American literature (Arthur Miller, Toni Morrison). Non-Anglophone translated foreign literature (Chekhov, Ibsen, Camus, Nam Le) represents ~10–15%. Australian literature (Oodgeroo Noonuccal, Alexis Wright, Henry Lawson, Patrick White) is protected by mandatory statutory quotas (*at least two Australian texts including Indigenous creators*).
4. **Concrete Finding**: NESA strictly enforces an Australian and Indigenous text quota: every HSC student must study a specified minimum number of Australian works, including Indigenous Australian creators (e.g., Oodgeroo Noonuccal, Jack Davis, Ivan Sen). This balances traditional British canonical mandates (such as mandatory Shakespeare in English Advanced) with indigenous and multicultural Australian voices.
   - **Source URL**: [https://educationstandards.nsw.edu.au](https://educationstandards.nsw.edu.au).
5. **Feasibility Grade**: **EASY**. NESA publishes an explicit, highly structured prescription catalog with exact editions and module assignments.

### 9. Switzerland (CH)

1. **Exact Document**:
   - **German Canton Example (Zürich)**: *Lehrplan 21 Kanton Zürich: Deutsch (Sekundarstufe I - 3. Zyklus)* & *Kantonsschule ZAP Lektüreempfehlungen*.
   - **French Canton Example (Vaud)**: *Plan d'études romand (PER): Français - Cycle 3 (9H–11H)*.
   - **Issuing Body**: Bildungsdirektion Kanton Zürich / EDK (Zürich); CIIP / DEF Canton de Vaud (Vaud).
   - **Most Recent Year**: 2017–2023 (Lehrplan 21 implementation) / 2011–2024 (PER implementation).
   - **Working URL**: [https://zh.lehrplan.ch](https://zh.lehrplan.ch) & [https://www.plandetudes.ch](https://www.plandetudes.ch).
2. **Format**: Cantonal competency-based frameworks; no single statutory master list exists at the federal or cantonal level (texts chosen by teachers/schools using regional teaching tools like *Sprachland / Sprachstarken* in ZH and MER in VD).
3. **Approximate Size & Foreign Share**:
   - Size: 0 statutory central titles (~10–15 works set by Gymnasium entry exams or local school *Fachschaften*).
   - Foreign share:
     - *Zürich (German-speaking)*: ~40–50% German national literature (Goethe, Schiller, Kafka) + Swiss-German icons (Max Frisch, Friedrich Dürrenmatt, Gottfried Keller), with ~20–30% translated foreign literature (French, English, world classics).
     - *Vaud (French-speaking)*: ~50–60% French national literature (Molière, Voltaire, Hugo) + Swiss-French classics (Rousseau, Ramuz), with ~15–20% translated foreign literature.
4. **Concrete Finding**: Article 62 of the Swiss Federal Constitution guarantees cantonal educational sovereignty. When the intercantonal *HarmoS Concordat* attempted to harmonize curricula via Lehrplan 21 (German cantons) and PER (French cantons), popular political initiatives were launched in cantons like Zürich, Aargau, and Lucerne seeking to block centralized reading lists and preserve local teacher autonomy.
   - **Source URL**: [https://www.lehrplan.ch](https://www.lehrplan.ch).
5. **Feasibility Grade**: **HARD**. Extreme cantonal decentralization; constructing a single table requires substituting cantonal Gymnasium entry recommended lists or official textbook anthologies.

### 10. Norway (NO)

1. **Exact Document**: *Læreplan i norsk (NOR01-07 - LK20 / Fagfornyelsen)* & *Norsk 10. trinn læreverk (Gyldendal Kontekst / Aschehoug Intertekst)*.
   - **Issuing Body**: Utdanningsdirektoratet (Udir) / Kunnskapsdepartementet.
   - **Most Recent Year**: 2020 / 2025 revision (NOR01-07).
   - **Working URL**: [https://www.udir.no/lk20/NOR01-07](https://www.udir.no/lk20/NOR01-07).
2. **Format**: Open competency-goal curriculum without a statutory list of mandatory titles.
3. **Approximate Size & Foreign Share**:
   - Size: 0 statutory titles in the national curriculum (~15–20 works per year selected via state-approved textbook anthologies like Gyldendal *Kontekst*).
   - Foreign share: ~20–30% of textbook anthologies consist of translated foreign works (Shakespeare, Mary Shelley, Kafka, Hemingway, modern global prose) alongside Norwegian icons (Henrik Ibsen, Bjørnstjerne Bjørnson, Alexander Kielland, Jonas Lie, Sigrid Undset, Tarjei Vesaas) and compulsory Indigenous Sámi literature in translation.
4. **Concrete Finding**: Under Education Minister Kristin Clemet and the LK06 reform (retained in LK20), Norway explicitly rejected establishing a statutory state-enforced "Norsk litterær kanon" (unlike Denmark's 2004 *Kanon for folkeskolen*). However, educational researchers found that textbook publishers (such as Gyldendal's *Kontekst*) created a "hidden canon" (*skjult kanon*), where the same core works (Ibsen's *A Doll's House*, Kielland's *Karen*, Hamsun's *Hunger*) are adopted by nearly all teachers nationwide anyway.
   - **Source URL**: [https://www.utdanningsnytt.no/fagartikkel-litteratur-norsk/den-skjulte-kanon-i-norskfaget/112450](https://www.utdanningsnytt.no/fagartikkel-litteratur-norsk/den-skjulte-kanon-i-norskfaget/112450).
5. **Feasibility Grade**: **HARD**. No statutory master list of titles; evaluation relies on textbook anthologies like Gyldendal *Kontekst* as proxy receipts.

## Wave-2 Pilot Recommendation

**Recommendation: Table-ize Brazil (BR / FUVEST) and Vietnam (VN) first.**

### Why these two?

1. **Brazil (BR / FUVEST)** provides the sharpest **"Closed Lusophone Window"** contrast in the entire global corpus. FUVEST's 9-work examineable list is published annually with explicit receipts. Crucially, it admits **0% non-Portuguese foreign literature**, but reserves **~22% of its list for Lusophone foreign authors** (Mozambique, Portugal). Furthermore, FUVEST's 2026 **100% female author mandate** represents one of the most striking, receipted curriculum reform events in the dataset.
2. **Vietnam (VN)** offers the ideal **"Statutory Multi-Polar Window"** counterpart. Under Circular 32/2018, Vietnam combines 6 statutory national masterworks with a mandatory foreign literature quota requiring representation from **8 distinct foreign national traditions** (UK, France, USA, Greece, Russia, Japan, China, India). This provides a rich, multi-polar Asian window directly comparable to China and Ukraine.
