# Kazakhstan: Two Appendices Each

In an ordinary Kazakh-medium or Russian-medium school in Kazakhstan, a ninth-grader carries
two literature-bearing lines on the timetable, and which two depends on one administrative
fact: the language the school teaches in. Both streams read literature in Kazakh and in
Russian; neither reads the same shelf. The window on the world's writing opens in a
different wall of each building, and one of the two streams meets no foreign author at all
in the subject called Literature.

That is not a paradox. It is the architecture of a single document.

## The door: one order, four of its appendices

Everything below comes from one instrument — Order No. 399 of the Minister of Education
(Просвещение) of the Republic of Kazakhstan, 16 September 2022, «Об утверждении типовых
учебных программ по общеобразовательным предметам…», registered with the Ministry of
Justice on 23 September 2022 under No. 29767, and amended in 2023 by Order No. 328 of
31 October — Appendices 36, 40 and 47 amended point by point, Appendix 48 reissued whole.
It approves model programs («типовые учебные программы») as numbered appendices, one per
subject.

More than four of those appendices carry literature at grade 9. Alongside the four read
here, the order approves Appendix 38, «Қазақ әдебиеті» for the specialized network of
«Абай мектептері» with deepened study of Kazakh language and literature, and Appendices 42,
44 and 46 — Uyghur, Uzbek and Tajik literature, for schools teaching in those languages.
None of those four is in this file, and nothing below is a statement about the country's
programs as a whole. What follows describes four appendices, which pair off by the school's
language of instruction:

- **Appendix 36 — "Казахская литература"**, for schools «с казахским языком обучения».
- **Appendix 40 — "Русская литература"**, for schools «с русским языком обучения».
- **Appendix 47 — "Казахский язык и литература"**, for schools «с неказахским языком
  обучения».
- **Appendix 48 — "Русский язык и литература"**, for schools «с нерусским языком обучения».

So a Kazakh-medium ninth-grader takes Appendix 36 and Appendix 48; a Russian-medium
ninth-grader takes Appendix 40 and Appendix 47. Each pair is one literature subject in the
language of the school and one language-and-literature subject in the other language. A
Uyghur-, Uzbek- or Tajik-medium ninth-grader falls outside that symmetry entirely — being
neither Kazakh-medium nor Russian-medium, they take Appendix 47 *and* Appendix 48, plus a
literature appendix of their own that this file does not hold.

The clock is where this order gets complicated, and it is why no hours are quoted below.
Appendices 36, 40 and 47 each print their grade-9 program twice — a full chapter and a
chapter «с сокращением учебной нагрузки», the reduced one carrying a lighter weekly load
and the same grade-9 works. Appendix 48 prints its plan twice as well, not by load but by
audience: a second long-term plan, with the identical grade-9 work list, for schools
teaching «с уйгурским/ узбекским/ таджикским языком обучения». Which version a given
classroom runs is settled somewhere else — by the типовой учебный план, a separate
instrument this order names and does not contain.

Force differs by appendix, and it is legible from the column headings. Appendices 36 and 40
head their work column «Изучаемые произведения» — works studied — in the full and the
reduced chapter alike, and Appendix 36 also prints a flat numbered list of the works at
point 20 of its text. Appendix 48 heads the same column «Изучаемые художественные
произведения (по выбору)» — literary works studied (by choice) — in both of its plans.
Appendix 47 is the one that will not hold still: its full-load plan heads that column
«Художественные произведения», with no «по выбору» anywhere in it, while its reduced-load
plan heads the identical grade-9 list «Изучаемые художественные произведения (по выбору)».
Same works, same order, two headings. The file's Appendix 47 rows are transcribed from the
full-load plan and coded `choice-list` from the reduced one, so the essay makes no claim
about how hard that appendix presses; and the file's totals — 32 rows `mandatory`, 30
`choice-list` — carry that unresolved mismatch in ten of the thirty. What none of these
rows is, is an exam item. Nothing here is a codifier; these are ministry programs, and a
row's force is the force of a printed program line, no more.

## What comes through

The repaired file holds 62 grade-9 rows: 35 domestic, 27 translated. Of the 27, twenty-one
are `translated:russian`, four `translated:english`, two `translated:kyrgyz` — both Aitmatov.

That is the file, and the file is not the whole shelf. Appendices 47 and 48 name works at
grade 9 that were never transcribed into it — they are listed in Limits — so every count in
this essay is a count of the file, not of the order. It is worth saying flatly what this
essay will not do with those counts either: the file records that these works are printed
in these programs, and nothing about why. Inheritance, language policy, the esteem of a
literature, the plain availability of translations and teachers — the table cannot tell
those apart, and no number below should be read as choosing among them.

The distribution across the four doors is the finding, not the totals.

Appendix 36, the Kazakh literature subject, holds 19 rows in grade 9 — the whole of the
document's grade-9 list — and every one is domestic: Balasagun's «Құтты білік» at the
eleventh century, the oratorical words of Tole Bi, Kazybek Bi and Aiteke Bi, Bukhar Zhyrau,
the aytys of Birzhan and Sara, Makhambet, Walikhanov's «Ыстықкөл күнделігі», Zhansugirov's
«Құлагер», Musirepov's «Улпан ее имя» — the one title the Kazakh literature list prints in
Russian — out to Aytuly's «Бәйтерек». No foreign author enters that subject at this grade.
Appendix 40, the Russian literature subject, holds 14 rows, again the document's full
grade-9 list, and every one is translated: thirteen Russian and Shakespeare's sonnets. The
two subjects that call themselves Literature are, at grade 9, one wholly domestic and one
wholly foreign.

The menus behave differently, and they are the two the file does not hold entire. Appendix
47 runs 10 rows here — nine domestic, from the Kul Tegin stele to a poem on the
Constitution — and one foreign: Asimov. Its plan also names Medeu Sarseke, an excerpt from
«Бес арыс» and an Abai text, all domestic, which no row records. Appendix 48 runs 19 rows,
seven domestic and twelve translated (eight Russian, two English, two Kyrgyz), all
`choice-list`; its plan names more than that, and every foreign work among the missing is
Russian. Which locates the thing worth locating: a Kazakh-medium ninth-grader's foreign
window is cut in the Russian-language course, not in the subject called Kazakh Literature —
and Appendix 48 heads its list «(по выбору)», so what stands in that window is offered
rather than required.

None of this is an openness measurement, and the arithmetic will not support one: 19 against
14 is a comparison between two differently-built subjects, not a score. It is a statement
about where in the building the window was cut.

A caution about the `origin` column while the shelf is in view. It codes an author's
literature, not the language of the copy a class opens. Mukhtar Shakhanov's «Четыре матери»
and Auezov's «Путь Абая» are coded `domestic` and appear in Appendix 48 under Russian
titles, read in Russian. Vladimir Gundarev's Russian-language «На земле Абая» is coded
`domestic` too, while Pavel Vasilyev's poems, in Appendix 40, are coded `translated:russian`.
Both Aitmatov rows are coded `translated:kyrgyz`; the corpus records no language of
composition, so nothing here should be read as a claim about which language either Aitmatov
text was written in. And Gogol — Ukrainian-born, Russian-language, a contested placement in
other countries' programs — arrives twice in Appendix 40 with no annotation of any kind.
The document places him and says nothing.

## The errand: four quarters, twice

The clearest teaching language in this order is the quarter headings, and reading the two
literature subjects side by side is the closest thing to a close-read the document permits.

Appendix 36 gives its Kazakh-medium ninth-graders: «Слова назидения мудрых» (words of
edification of the wise), «Историческая правда и художественное решение» (historical truth
and its artistic resolution), «Секреты человеческой души» (secrets of the human soul),
«Мәңгілік Ел – вечная цель» (Mangilik El — the eternal goal).

Appendix 40 gives its Russian-medium ninth-graders: «Странности любви» (the strangenesses
of love), «Тема маленького человека» (the theme of the little man), «Сатира на общество»
(satire on society), «Тайны человеческой души» (mysteries of the human soul).

The third and fourth headings rhyme almost to the word — секреты and тайны, the same organ
of feeling opened in each stream — and are filled from opposite shelves. Kazakh literature
gets to the human soul through Zhansugirov, Mailin, Musirepov, Aybergenov and Ongarsynova;
Russian literature gets there through «Пиковая дама», Chekhov and Leskov. Two ministries of
feeling in one ministry, running the same agenda with no author in common.

The quarter that has no counterpart is «Тема маленького человека». Under it: Gogol's
«Шинель», Dostoevsky's «Бедные люди», Tolstoy's «После бала», Kuprin's «Гранатовый браслет».
An overcoat, a clerk's letters, a flogging watched from the edge of a ballroom, a telegraph
official's unanswerable love. The heading names its own errand — a category of person, and a
practised way of feeling about that person, imported entire as one quarter of a school year.
The corpus's own repair pass had to correct three of these rows from `serfdom?` and
`tsarist-expansion?` to urban poverty, bureaucracy, and military punishment; read together —
and this reading is the essay's, assembled from those reconciled `subject_era` codings, not
a rationale the ministry anywhere offers — the wounds the quarter carries are the empire's
own, and the empire's own literature does the carrying.

Appendix 48's headings are built on a different principle again, because it is a language
course: «Культура народов мира», «Природные ресурсы планеты Земля», «История и личность»,
«Реальность и фантазия», «Отцы и дети: диалог и конфликт поколений», «Молодежь и средства
массовой информации», «Мир един: глобализация», «Знаменитые люди», «Я и закон». Literature
here is material for a theme. Under «Я и закон» — me and the law — the text listed for the
unit is «Ч. Айтматов. "Плаха" (фрагмент самосуда Бостона)», the Boston fragment of a novel,
offered as the reading for a unit on legality. Under «Реальность и фантазия», Aitmatov's
horned mother deer sits beside Bradbury's «Все лето в один день». Under «Знаменитые люди»,
Lermontov's «Смерть поэта».

One objective states the intended triangulation outright. Appendix 40, third quarter,
objective 9.4.3.1: «сопоставлять произведения (или фрагменты) русской, казахской и мировой
литературы, близкие по тематике/ проблематике/жанру, учитывая особенности национальной
культуры» — "to compare works (or fragments) of Russian, Kazakh and world literature that
are close in theme, issues or genre, taking account of the features of national culture."
The subject's stated task, in Chapter 1, is to build competence «на основе русской,
казахской и мировой литературы и культуры» — on the basis of Russian, Kazakh and world
literature and culture. Three terms. Appendix 36's comparative objective, 9.3.4.1, has two:
«проанализировав литературное произведение в сопоставлении с образцами казахской и мировой
литературы, написать небольшую критическую статью» — analyze a work against examples of
Kazakh and world literature, and write a short critical article. In the Kazakh literature
subject, Russian literature is not named as a separate term; it is inside the world. In the
Russian literature subject, it is beside it.

## What a body is required to hold

Twelve rows in this file carry a memorization mandate, coded `excerpt`. All twelve are in
Appendix 40. All twelve are translated — eleven Russian, plus Shakespeare's sonnets. No
domestic row in the file carries one.

The mandate is objective 9.1.3.1, attached to the first, second and fourth quarters of
Appendix 40's grade-9 plan: «самостоятельно находить в тексте и выразительно читать
наизусть цитаты, фрагменты, отражающие тематику произведения» — "independently find in the
text and expressively recite from memory quotations, fragments reflecting the work's
theme." The locating is to be done independently and the lines are to be carried in the
body. Who settles on which passage, and how any of it is run in a room, the objective does
not say, and neither will this. The third quarter, Griboyedov and «Мертвые души», does not
carry the objective, which is why those two rows read `no`.

If your classroom made you learn something by heart, you can probably still start the poem.
What this order writes down is narrower than that memory: an explicit by-heart objective, in
one of these four appendices only, attached to books that all come from somewhere else.

That sentence needs its brake fitted immediately. This is a fact about where an explicit
by-heart objective is *written*, not a claim about which poems Kazakhstani children actually
memorize. Appendix 36 asks instead for «использование отрывков из художественных
произведений в творческих работах» — the use of excerpts in creative work — and the
reconciliation pass stripped four earlier `excerpt` codings from its rows for exactly that
reason. Recitation culture around Abai and Makhambet is not in this file, and its absence
from the file is not evidence of its absence from a classroom.

## The one that came through the other door

Across the two Kazakh-language programs in this file — Appendix 36 and Appendix 47 — a
single foreign work appears at grade 9, under the fourth-quarter theme «Мир роботов и
киборгов», the world of robots and cyborgs. The full-load plan prints the text as «Айзек
Азимов "Мен-роботпын" (отрывок из сборника)»; the reduced-load plan prints the same slot in
Russian, «Айзек Азимов "Я -робот"». An American's robot stories, under a heading about
machines, in the course a Russian-medium child takes to learn the state language — and in
the fuller of the two plans, in Kazakh.

Set the two streams against each other one last time and the shape is plain. One
ninth-grader meets, in the subject called Literature, nineteen domestic works and no
foreigner at all, and meets the world through a Russian-language course whose list is headed
«(по выбору)» — offered, not required. The other meets, in the subject called Literature,
fourteen works of which every one is translated and twelve carry an explicit by-heart
objective, and meets Kazakh through a course where the world arrives once, as Asimov. Same
order, same year, same ministry, same signature. Two windows, cut in different walls of one
house.

## Limits

- **This is four appendices, not a country.** The same order approves Appendix 38
  («Қазақ әдебиеті» for the specialized «Абай мектептері» network) and Appendices 42, 44
  and 46 (Uyghur, Uzbek and Tajik literature, grades 5–9). None was transcribed, so nothing
  above describes what a pupil in an Abai school or a Uyghur-, Uzbek- or Tajik-medium school
  reads in their own literature subject.
- **Load variants are left out, and one of them is unresolved.** Appendices 36, 40 and 47
  each publish a full-load and a reduced-load grade-9 program; Appendix 48 publishes a second
  plan for Uyghur/Uzbek/Tajik-medium schools. Only one variant per appendix was transcribed
  (Receipts identifies which). The grade-9 work lists agree across variants, but Appendix 47's
  two head their work column differently, and its ten rows take their titles from one variant
  and their `choice-list` coding from the other. Until that is re-receipted those ten of the
  file's thirty `choice-list` rows are not settled, and no force claim is made about Appendix
  47 above. No hours are quoted anywhere in the body: the load points give different figures
  for different variants, and the corpus has no hours field.
- **The table is not a complete transcription of two of the four appendices.** Appendices 36
  (19 rows) and 40 (14 rows) match the document's grade-9 lists exactly. Appendices 47 and 48
  do not. Named in the document's grade-9 plans with no row in the file: Appendix 48 —
  Paustovsky «Мещерская сторона», Rasputin «Прощание с Матерой», Seifullin «Кокшетау»,
  Sasha Chorny «Диета»/«Зеркало», Akunin «Турецкий гамбит», Abai «Не будь падким на все»,
  the first-quarter «Плаха» excerpt listed separately from the fourth-quarter one, and the
  alternative «Просчет Чингисхана» offered beside Shakhanov's Otrar poem; Appendix 47 —
  Medeu Sarseke «Жетінші толқын», the «Бес арыс» excerpt, Abai «Адамның кейбір кездері».
  Every foreign work among them is Russian, so the file's Russian majority is not produced by
  over-collection — but 21-of-27, and every other count above, is a count of the repaired
  file, not of the order.
- **One grade, one year.** Grade 9 only, in the 2023 redaction. Nothing here describes what
  a Kazakhstani pupil reads across a school career, and no reform history is receipted: the
  corpus holds no earlier Kazakhstani list, so no row can be dated as added or removed.
- **These are programs, not an exam list.** `exam_status` is the compiler's coding of a
  program's own wording — a work column headed «Изучаемые произведения» versus one headed
  «Изучаемые художественные произведения (по выбору)» — and not a statement of
  examinability. No count here is comparable with a country whose door is an exam codifier.
- **`origin` codes literature, not language.** Russian-language works by authors coded
  `domestic` (Gundarev), Kazakh works read in Russian translation (Shakhanov, Auezov,
  Baitursynov), and both Aitmatov rows coded `translated:kyrgyz` all sit in the same column.
  The corpus records no language of composition and no birthplace; nothing above should be
  read as a claim about either.
- **Memorization codings are conservative, and one-sided by construction.** `excerpt`
  required an explicit by-heart mandate attached to the listed work. Only Appendix 40
  attaches one (9.1.3.1). Appendices 36, 47 and 48 assign reading, discussion, quotation and
  creative use of passages, which the ruling does not count. The finding is therefore about
  where a by-heart objective is written into a program, and is not evidence about what is
  learned by heart in either stream, or about who selects the passage.
- **No motive, anywhere.** The document states subjects, themes and objectives. It offers no
  rationale for the composition of any shelf, and none was located in `resources/`. The 21
  Russian rows are an admission recorded in a program; this essay makes no claim about why
  they are there.
