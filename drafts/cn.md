# China: The Mirror on the Shelf

Somewhere in the compulsory years of a Chinese schooling — the document that would say
exactly when is not in this file — a pupil is set a habit rather than a book: the
curriculum standard requires that students 「多读书、读好书、读整本书」 — "read more,
read good books, read whole books" — and, for the long ones, offers a single example
sentence. It is worth walking like a shelf, left to right:

> 「长篇名著，如吴承恩《西游记》，施耐庵《水浒传》，老舍《骆驼祥子》，罗广斌、杨益言《红岩》，埃德加·斯诺《红星照耀中国》，斯威夫特《格列佛游记》，夏洛蒂·勃朗特《简·爱》，高尔基《童年》，奥斯特洛夫斯基《钢铁是怎样炼成的》等。」 (p. 64)

Nine titles. The first four are Chinese: Wu Cheng'en, Shi Nai'an, Lao She, then Luo
Guangbin and Yang Yiyan's 《红岩》. The last four are foreign-authored: Swift, Brontë,
Gorky, Ostrovsky. Between the halves, fifth of nine, stands 埃德加·斯诺《红星照耀中国》
— Edgar Snow, whose book this table codes `translated:english`, and whose subject is
China: an account of China, written from outside it, in another language. A window is
supposed to face outward. This one has been hung facing the room.

The corpus under this essay is narrower than the printed line, and the seam should be
visible: of the line's five foreign titles, three carry rows here — Snow, Brontë,
Ostrovsky — while Swift and Gorky do not (Limits says why); the fourth foreign row,
Jules Verne, is printed on a different line of the same appendix. Four translated rows
against sixty-five domestic — a shelf small enough to read closely.

## The door, which has two leaves

The defining document is 《义务教育语文课程标准（2022年版）》 — the Compulsory Education
Chinese Language Curriculum Standard, 2022 edition — issued by the Ministry of Education
of the People's Republic of China in April 2022. It governs one subject, 语文; this
table holds its lower-secondary rows. It names books in two appendices with unlike
force.

Appendix 1 is the recitation leaf, and the window arrives there before any foreign book
does — inside a subordinate clause of the sentence that establishes the mandate:

> 「义务教育阶段要求学生背诵古今优秀诗文，包括中国古代、现当代和外国优秀诗文，具体篇目可由教科书编者和语文教师推荐，这里仅推荐古诗文135篇（段）。」
> — "The compulsory-education stage requires students to recite excellent poetry and prose
> ancient and modern, including Chinese classical, Chinese modern and contemporary, and
> foreign excellent poetry and prose; the specific pieces may be recommended by textbook
> editors and language teachers; here only 135 pieces (passages) of classical poetry and
> prose are recommended." (Appendix 1, headnote, printed p. 58; my translation)

The clause opens a Chinese teenager's memory to the poetry of the world — then the list
underneath names classical Chinese poetry and prose and hands the specific pieces to
editors and teachers. Of its Grade 7–9 segment — sixty rows in this table — the number
that are foreign is zero. The foreigners are all on the other leaf.

Appendix 2, the reading leaf — 关于课内外读物的建议, "Suggestions concerning reading
materials in and out of class" — opens by requiring the habit quoted above, then
disclaims its own catalogue in the next paragraph: 「下列推荐的读物仅为举例」 — "the
reading materials recommended below are examples only" (p. 64). Neither leaf makes a
named work examinable, so the reconciliation pass codes every row `choice-list`. A
row's force in China is not examinability but a named example inside a mandated
practice: the state requires that reciting happen and that whole books be read, and
leaves to editors and teachers which texts do the work.

The nine whole-book rows this table retains — five domestic, four translated, a set
that is *not* the printed nine-title line — cite a second source for their grade
placement: the 名著导读 ("guided reading of masterworks") units of the People's
Education Press unified textbook, whose pages are not in `resources/`; Appendix 2
assigns no grades at all. So this essay makes no use of the grade column, and the pupil
in the opening went ungraded for exactly that reason. Nor is four foreign works read as
a measurement of welcome: an example list and an examination codifier are different
instruments. What the four rows carry is a filing — and the filing is the document's
own.

## The guests, in the document's own categories

Sixty-nine rows: sixty-five domestic, four translated — two from English, one from
French, one from Russian. Every foreign row sits in Appendix 2, under two of its
headings.

Under 科普科幻作品 — "popular-science and science-fiction works" — stands one line:
「科普科幻作品，如《十万个为什么》、儒勒·凡尔纳《海底两万里》等。」 (p. 64). Jules Verne
(1828–1905) enters beside 《十万个为什么》 — "A Hundred Thousand Whys" — not among the
masterworks but on the line shared with popular science. That heading covers science
fiction expressly, so nothing here says the ministry declines to call the book a novel;
what the filing says is what stands next to it. Reading that placement — this essay's
reading, not a stated purpose — a submarine arrives as apparatus: a book admitted in
the company of explanations.

The other two rows stand on the masterworks line already walked: Charlotte Brontë
(1816–1855) supplies 《简·爱》, its subject era coded `19th-c-victorian-class-gender`;
Nikolai Ostrovsky (1904–1936), coded `translated:russian`, supplies
《钢铁是怎样炼成的》, coded `russian-civil-war` — the one row on this shelf whose coded
subject is a war fought outside China.

Ostrovsky's is the row worth opening, because the feeling it imports announces itself
on the page — in the novel's most famous passage, the one that made it a school text
across the Soviet world: «Самое дорогое у человека — это жизнь. Она даётся ему один
раз, и прожить её надо так, чтобы не было мучительно больно за бесцельно прожитые годы»
— "The most precious thing a person has is life. It is given once, and it must be lived
so that there is no torturing pain over years spent without purpose." A steelworks of
the will, offered at the age when years first look spendable. That the feeling on this
shelf's one foreign war-book is not grief but resolve is this essay's reading of the
work's own sentence; the standard prints only the title.

## Edgar Snow, the mirror

And the fifth position. Snow (1905–1972) is the only foreign row not coded as fiction:
`genre` reads `essay`, `subject_era` reads `red-army-long-march`. Three things about
this row can be read closely, and only three.

First, the heading. 名著 is canon vocabulary — the established, famous work. The
appendix keeps a separate line for 诗歌散文作品, poetry-and-prose works, where Lu Xun's
《朝花夕拾》 stands; Snow's book is on the masterworks line instead, wedged between the
Chinese novels and the foreign ones, and the table's `essay` coding records the
mismatch: a book of reporting filed among the classics.

Second, the title. The Chinese does something the English gloss does not. *Red Star
Over China* is a preposition — a star hanging in place. 《红星照耀中国》 puts a verb in
the middle: 照耀, to shine upon, to illuminate. The star acts, and the country is the
grammatical object.

Third, an adjacency. The appendix's one stated direction about content — that teachers
recommending *supplementary* reading should favor 「特别是反映革命文化和社会主义先进文化
的作品」, "especially works reflecting revolutionary culture and advanced socialist
culture" (p. 64) — attaches to supplementary recommending in general, not to the
masterworks line, and not to Snow. The document never connects the two, and neither
does this essay; the table can say only that Snow's is the one foreign row whose coded
subject era belongs to the history that instruction names.

So the payoff stays at the level of the page. A pupil who walks the printed line meets,
in the middle of it, a foreign witness filed among the masters, whose Chinese title has
the star illuminating the country itself. The effect of that juxtaposition — a window
hung facing the room — is this essay's reading of a placement and a grammar. Whatever
reason the ministry has, it is not in the corpus.

## What is held, and what is only read

The table's `memorize` column reads `no` on all sixty-nine rows: the reconciliation
pass required an explicit mandate to learn a *named* text by heart, and Appendix 1's
headnote requires recitation in general, calls its pieces recommendations, and leaves
the specific pieces to editors and teachers. No single work in this table is a mandated
memory.

But look where the two leaves point. The recommended recitation shelf — Guan Ju, Mulan,
Du Fu's spring, Wen Tianxiang passing Lingding Ocean — is Chinese without exception
across its sixty Grade 7–9 rows; the corpus's whole-book shelf is nine rows, five
Chinese and four foreign. On this national list, no foreign work is named for memory,
and four are named for reading. And the clause that opened recitation to 外国优秀诗文
stands open, its filling handed to editors and teachers; whatever they choose, this
corpus never sees. A foreign poem may already be in Chinese children's mouths by the
route the document describes and declines to name.

If your classroom set a list of whole books for the year, run the Chinese test on it:
which was written by an author from outside your country, and which of those was about
your country anyway. This corpus produces the second kind exactly once — and hangs it
in the middle of the state's own example sentence.

## Limits

- **Four rows are not this country's whole foreign presence.** The 长篇名著 line prints
  five foreign-authored titles; Swift's 《格列佛游记》 and Gorky's 《童年》 carry no row
  because no lower-secondary placement for them is receipted, and the fairy-tale and
  fable lines name 安徒生童话, 格林童话 and 《伊索寓言》, likewise rowless. The four
  rows are the works with a claimed lower-secondary placement, not the ceiling of what
  the appendix names.
- **The quarantine, stated plainly.** Three rows were removed before this essay was
  written: Jean-Henri Fabre's 《昆虫记》, Fu Lei's 《傅雷家书》, and Wu Jingzi's
  《儒林外史》 — each searched for under Chinese and Latin author and title variants
  across every saved defining document and found in none, with no textbook receipt in
  `resources/`. They stay out until a work-level receipt exists; no count above includes
  them. This is not evidence they are absent from Chinese classrooms — only that this
  corpus cannot receipt them.
- **The nine whole-book rows lean on an unsaved receipt.** Their titles are corroborated
  by saved Appendix 2; their *grade placements* come from the PEP unified textbook,
  whose pages are not in `resources/`. The `grade` column is unreceipted and nothing
  above rests on it; the opening pupil carries no grade for exactly this reason.
- **Force is coded, not measured.** All 69 rows read `choice-list`: Appendix 2 says of
  its catalogue 「仅为举例」 — "examples only" — and Appendix 1 calls its pieces
  recommendations. A coding says a work is named in a binding document, not that a
  student can be tested on it; no cross-country comparison of row totals is offered.
- **The recitation clause's delegated choices are unobserved.** Editor- and
  teacher-selected recitation texts — where foreign work may well already sit — are
  outside this corpus; the memory finding is about what this list names, not what
  Chinese pupils recite.
- **One slice, one subject.** The 2022 standard as of a 2026-08-10 retrieval, 语文
  only. Foreign works inside the English-language subject, provincial supplementary
  lists, or senior secondary (a different standard) are outside this table.
- **Motive is not in the corpus.** The appendix names a direction for supplementary
  recommendation and places Snow's book on the masterworks line; it does not connect
  the two, and neither does this essay. The submarine-as-apparatus, resolve-not-grief,
  and window-hung-facing-the-room readings are this essay's — placements, grammar, one
  quoted passage — not purposes the ministry states. Placement is the finding.
